What we do

Four attachments.
One very capable boat.

Everything is mechanical harvesting — we physically remove the plant and the biomass leaves with us. Nothing is sprayed, and nothing is left to rot on the bottom and feed next year's growth.

01

Aquatic weed harvesting

The core service. A cutter bar shears vegetation at the root crown while the onboard basket collects it, so the cuttings never drift back into your cove. Ideal for milfoil, hydrilla and water chestnut mats.

  • Cuts in as little as 18" of water
  • Collects as it cuts
  • Off-site disposal included
02

Root rake & cattails

A hydraulic rake pulls cattail root balls, stumps and storm-downed branches clean out of the shallows — the stuff a cutter bar can't touch.

03

Algae skimming

A surface skimmer attachment lifts floating filamentous algae and duckweed off the top of the water before it blows onto your beach.

04

Shoreline pole saw

Overhanging limbs trimmed from the water side, where a ladder and a chainsaw have no business being.

How it works

Three steps, one season's fix.

  1. 1

    We look at the water

    Send photos and a rough idea of the area, or we come out. We identify what's actually growing — the plan for water chestnut is not the plan for lily pads — and give you a flat quote.

  2. 2

    We harvest

    The Weedoo launches from your shoreline or the nearest access. It cuts, collects, and shuttles full baskets to a staging point on shore. Most residential frontages are a one- or two-day job.

  3. 3

    It leaves with us

    Biomass is loaded out and hauled away — not piled at your treeline. You get open water, a clean shoreline, and no nutrient load going back into the lake.

Know your enemy

What we pull out of Hudson Valley water.

Eurasian Watermilfoil

Feathery, dense, and it spreads from a single broken fragment. Forms mats thick enough to stop a propeller. The reason most people call us.

Water Chestnut

Floating rosettes that blanket the surface and drop spiked nuts on your beach. Timing matters — we target it before the seed drops.

Hydrilla

Aggressive, fast, and regulated in New York. Grows from tubers, so mechanical removal is about staying ahead of it every season.

Lily Pads

Pretty in a photo, a wall in a canoe. We clear swim lanes and dock approaches and leave the rest as habitat if you want it.

Cattails

Root-bound and stubborn. This is a rake job, not a cutting job — we take the root ball so it doesn't march further into the pond.

Algae & Duckweed

Surface scum that shows up on the windward shore. Skimmed off the top and removed before it turns your waterfront green.
