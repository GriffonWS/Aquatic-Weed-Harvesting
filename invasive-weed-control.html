Invasive Aquatic Weed Control

Control invasive vegetation affecting public beaches, community lakes, boat launches, and shared waterfronts. We mechanically harvest unwanted growth, collect the cut vegetation, and remove the biomass from the water.

Who we work with

Invasive weed management for public and community waterbodies.

  • 01

    Municipal lake weed management

    Support ongoing vegetation management for town and village lakes, public ponds, parks, boat launches, and recreational waterfronts. We coordinate removal around public access, seasonal demand, and priority areas identified by the municipality.

    • Public lakes and ponds
    • Parks and recreation areas
    • Boat launches and access channels
    • Targeted seasonal harvesting
  • 02

    Public beach weed removal service

    Dense aquatic growth can affect swimming areas, shorelines, and how visitors experience a public beach. Our compact work boat targets vegetation in shallow and frequently used areas.

    • Swimming and wading areas
    • Beachfront shorelines
    • Access routes and safety zones
    • Scheduling around public use
  • 03

    Lake association weed harvesting

    Lake and homeowner associations often need to balance multiple properties, shared access points, and annual maintenance priorities. We work with association representatives to define treatment areas and create a harvesting plan for the waterbody.

    • Community swim areas
    • Shared docks and boat lanes
    • High-priority coves and shorelines
    • Seasonal maintenance programs

What we control

Invasive aquatic vegetation we control.

Eurasian watermilfoil removal

Our targeted Eurasian Watermilfoil removal services address dense underwater growth that impedes swimming, boating, and the health of native ecosystems. Through careful mechanical harvesting and complete collection of cut fragments, we prevent further spreading across your waterbody.

Water chestnut removal

We specialize in water chestnut removal to clear heavy floating mats restricting access to shorelines and open water. Our team schedules harvesting operations early in the season to extract vegetation before mature plants drop seeds.

Lake hydrilla eradication service

Our professional lake hydrilla eradication service targets aggressive, rapid submerged growth. We combine mechanical removal with structured, ongoing monitoring to manage recurrent growth and restore clarity to affected water bodies.

Additional vegetation management services

We also offer customized harvesting solutions for lily pads, cattails, algae mats, and other nuisance aquatic weeds.

The approach

Why use mechanical harvesting?

  • 01

    Immediate physical control

    Harvesting removes existing vegetation from the selected treatment area rather than waiting for it to break down.

  • 02

    Biomass removal

    Collected plant material is removed from the water rather than being left to decompose.

  • 03

    Targeted treatment areas

    Prioritize beaches, docks, navigation channels, launches, and other high-use areas, rather than treating the entire waterbody.

  • 04

    Flexible scheduling

    Coordinate work around public events, peak recreation periods, association requirements, and seasonal plant growth.

  • 05

    No herbicide application

    Mechanical harvesting controls vegetation without using aquatic herbicides. Any access requirements are reviewed based on the project and local regulations.

Before work begins

A clear scope for every project.

Municipal and association projects often involve public-use requirements and strict budget constraints. Before work begins, we establish a clear project scope that defines:

  • Priority treatment areas
  • Target vegetation
  • Estimated project schedule
  • Equipment access
  • Biomass handling and disposal
  • Required permits or approvals
  • Documentation requirements
  • Point of contact for project decisions

Questions

Invasive aquatic weed control questions.

Can you work around public beaches?

Yes. Treatment areas and scheduling can be coordinated around swimming areas, visitor access, events, and peak recreation periods.

Do you work with lake associations?

Yes. Projects can be scoped around shared docks, beaches, boat lanes, coves, and other association priorities.

Do you provide municipal lake weed management?

Yes. We support municipal vegetation-management programs for public lakes, ponds, parks, beaches, and boat launches.

Do you use aquatic herbicides?

No. We use mechanical equipment to cut, collect, and remove unwanted aquatic vegetation.

When should invasive weeds be harvested?

Timing depends on the species. Water chestnut, for example, should generally be removed before mature plants release seeds.
